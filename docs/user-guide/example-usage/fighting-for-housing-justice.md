# Fighting For Housing Justice

**The Challenge**

Housing organisers often need to understand who holds power over homes, estates, and land in a neighbourhood: whether that is a private landlord, developer, housing association, council, or another public body. The barrier is usually not just lack of evidence, but the difficulty of seeing the wider pattern. Looking up individual titles through HM Land Registry can be slow and expensive, and it does not easily show how apparently separate properties, blocks, or sites may be connected through one owner or portfolio.

**How Land Explorer Can Help**

Land Explorer lets housing organisers create a focused map of an estate, neighbourhood, planning site, housing association, or landlord portfolio, and explore ownership visually. The ownership layers help show property boundaries, freehold and leasehold arrangements, and whether land is held by a company or private individual where that information is available. The back search function is particularly useful when you already know one building, landlord, company, or housing association and want to see what else they own. Maps can also be saved and shared, making it easier for a group to build knowledge collectively rather than relying on one person to hold all the information.

**What It Can Reveal**

Land Explorer can reveal ownership patterns that are hard to see at street level. For example, it can show that several buildings are held under one title, that multiple properties are controlled by the same company, that a housing provider has a larger footprint in an area than expected, or that public land is concentrated in particular places. It can also help clarify who controls building-level decisions, who may benefit financially, and how individual housing issues connect to wider structures of land ownership and control.

**How Land Explorer Supports The Work**

Land Explorer can help housing organisers move from isolated cases to structural analysis. The information can support briefings for tenants, conversations with councillors, FOI requests, media work, campaign research, and deeper investigations into landlords, developers, or public bodies. It is especially useful for identifying patterns first, before deciding whether to buy official Land Registry documents for specific titles or parcels.&#x20;

**Resources To Use With Land Explorer**

Land Explorer becomes even more useful when used alongside a few other public research tools. It can help you identify a site, narrow down boundaries, find the relevant title or ownership pattern, and connect one property to a wider portfolio. From there, these additional resources can help you verify legal details, track planning activity, request missing public information, or investigate the companies behind housing, land, and development decisions.

* [HM Land Registry search ](https://search-property-information.service.gov.uk/)— Check current title registers, title plans, and filed deeds for a property or parcel.
* [Historic Land Registry search (HC1) ](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/historical-registertitle-plan-registration-hc1)— Request older editions of a register or title plan for a registered title.
* Your local authority planning register — Track planning applications, decisions, documents, and comment periods for a site. You can find your local register via GOV.UK and searching your postcode. Councils typically publish current applications, decision notices, and supporting documents, and some offer alerts or a tracking function.&#x20;
* [Campaign for Freedom of Information: short guide ](https://www.cfoi.org.uk/pdf/foi_guide.pdf)— Plain-language introduction to using FOI rights in practice.
* [WhatDoTheyKnow](http://whatdotheyknow.com) — Free platform for submitting, tracking, and publishing FOI requests.
* [Companies House](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/companies-house) — Check company directors, registered addresses, filing history, and linked entities.
* [Corporate Watch DIY guide](https://corporatewatch.org/investigating-companies-a-do-it-yourself-handbook/) — Step-by-step organiser’s guide to finding out who owns and runs a company, how it is structured, and what other information can be uncovered about its operations, finances, and political links.&#x20;
* [Right to Know ](https://right-to-know.org/)— Map and postcode tool for exploring pollution, emissions, landfill, and related environmental data.
* [ICIJ Offshore Leaks Database ](https://offshoreleaks.icij.org/)— Search offshore companies, trusts, and linked people from major leak datasets.
* [Violation Tracker UK](https://violationtrackeruk.goodjobsfirst.org/) — Search UK corporate enforcement actions and regulatory infringements across sectors.&#x20;

**Features To Use**

Ownership layers, freehold and leasehold layers, back search, search by company, collaborative mapping

**Tips**

Start with a clear focus, such as one estate, one landlord, one housing association, or one planning site, rather than trying to map everything at once. Use Land Explorer to identify patterns first, then decide which titles or Land Registry documents are worth following up. If you are investigating a company owner, Land Explorer works well alongside Companies House and other research tools.
